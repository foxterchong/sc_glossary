Classification 1: Earth Structure & Landforms (地球結構與地貌)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Crust 地殼 Ch 1The Earth's outermost solid layer made of rock plates.
Mantle 地幔 Ch 1Middle layer between the crust and core containing hot flowing magma.
Core 地核 Ch 1The extremely hot centre of the Earth mainly made up of metals under high pressure.
Outer Core 外地核 Ch 1Fluid metallic layer surrounding the innermost core beneath the mantle.
Inner Core 內地核 Ch 1Central hottest region of the Earth under intense pressure and heat.
Plate 板塊 Ch 1Large rock sections of the crust that slowly move over the mantle.
Crustal Plate 地殼板塊 Ch 1A major segment of Earth's crust moving relative to neighboring plates.
Plate Movement 板塊移動 Ch 1Slow shifting of crustal plates driven by magma flow beneath them.
Plate Collision 板塊碰撞 Ch 1Crashing together of Earth's plates that can cause earthquakes or uplift land.
Magma 岩漿 Ch 1Very hot liquid rock inside the Earth that forms igneous rocks when cooled.
Flowing Magma 流動岩漿 Ch 1Hot molten rock moving within the mantle and influencing plate motion.
Earthquake 地震 Ch 1Sudden shaking of the ground caused by plate movement, rubbing, or collision.
Volcanic Eruption 火山爆發 Ch 1Natural event in which hot magma reaches the Earth's surface through vents or cracks.
Landform 地貌 Ch 1Natural surface feature of the Earth such as mountains, valleys, or plains.
Ocean 海洋 Ch 1Vast body of saltwater covering large parts of the Earth's crust.
Lake 湖泊 Ch 1Body of water surrounded by land on the Earth's crust.
River 河流 Ch 1Natural flowing stream of water moving across land toward a sea, lake, or ocean.
High Pressure 高壓 Ch 1Strong squeezing force deep underground that compacts sediments or forms metamorphic rock.
High Temperature 高溫 Ch 1Very hot thermal condition found deep inside the Earth or during volcanic activity.
Earth's Surface 地球表面 Ch 2Outer face of the Earth where land, oceans, and air meet.

Classification 2: Rocks & Soil (岩石與土壤)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Igneous Rock 火成岩 Ch 1Rock formed when magma cools and hardens underground or on the surface.
Sedimentary Rock 沉積岩 Ch 1Rock formed by rock pieces and soil piling up and pressing together over time.
Metamorphic Rock 變質岩 Ch 1Rock formed when existing rocks change structure under high heat and pressure.
Granite 花崗岩 Ch 1Hard igneous rock cooled slowly underground with visible larger mineral grains.
Basalt 玄武岩 Ch 1Igneous rock formed when magma cools quickly on the ground; fine grains, dark colour.
Sandstone 砂岩 Ch 1Sedimentary rock formed from compacted sand layers, often light brown.
Shale 頁岩 Ch 1Fine-grained sedimentary rock formed from clay sediments with thin visible layers.
Limestone 石灰岩 Ch 1Sedimentary rock that can transform into marble under high heat and pressure.
Marble 大理岩 Ch 1Metamorphic rock formed from limestone; often white or milky white with mineral patterns.
Gneiss 片麻岩 Ch 1Metamorphic rock formed from granite under high heat and pressure with striped bands.
Rock Grain 岩石顆粒 Ch 1Individual mineral or rock particles making up the texture of a rock.
Grain Size 顆粒大小 Ch 1Characteristic describing whether grains in a rock or soil are large, medium, or fine.
Rock Hardness 岩石硬度 Ch 1Resistance of a rock to being scratched or worn down by other objects.
Mineral 礦物 Ch 1Naturally occurring solid inorganic substance that makes up rocks.
Sedimentation 沉積作用 Ch 1Process in which transported rock fragments and soil settle into layers.
Weathering 風化作用 Ch 1Breaking down of rocks into smaller pieces and soil by wind, rain, and temperature changes.
Soil 土壤 Ch 1Upper layer of Earth supporting plant growth, formed from weathered rock and organic matter.
Soil Particle 土壤顆粒 Ch 1Solid mineral bits of varying sizes found within soil.
Organic Matter 有機物 Ch 1Decaying or remaining animal and plant material present in soil.
Sandy Soil 沙土 Ch 1Soil with large particles and wide spaces; drains water quickly, suitable for cacti.
Loam 壤土 Ch 1Soil with balanced particle size, air flow, and drainage; good for many plants like roses.
Clay Soil 黏土 Ch 1Soil with very small particles and tight packing; low drainage and easily waterlogged.
Drainage Capacity 排水能力 Ch 1How quickly water passes through soil based on particle size and spacing.
Waterlogged 積水／水浸 Ch 1Condition where soil is saturated with excess water and lacks sufficient air.

Classification 3: Astronomy & Motion (天文與運動)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
North Pole 北極 Ch 2Northernmost point on Earth, in the Arctic Ocean and covered with sea ice year-round.
South Pole 南極 Ch 2Southernmost point on Earth, located in Antarctica, the coldest continent.
Earth's Axis 地軸 Ch 2Tilted imaginary straight line passing through the North Pole and South Pole.
Imaginary Line 假想線 Ch 2Conceptual reference line such as the Earth's axis or equator used in geography.
Tilted Axis 地軸傾斜 Ch 2Inclination of Earth's rotation axis that leads to changing sunlight angles and seasons.
Rotation 自轉 Ch 2Spinning movement of the Earth on its axis from west to east.
Rotate 自轉／旋轉 Ch 2To spin around an internal axis, such as Earth completing one rotation in 24 hours.
Twenty-four Hours 二十四小時 Ch 2Approximate time taken for Earth to complete one full rotation, equal to one day.
Day 白天／日 Ch 2Part of the 24-hour cycle when a place faces toward the Sun and receives daylight.
Night 黑夜／夜 Ch 2Part of the 24-hour cycle when a place faces away from the Sun.
Day and Night Cycle 晝夜交替 Ch 2Recurring pattern of day and night caused by Earth's rotation on its axis.
Sunlight 陽光 Ch 2Light and heat energy emitted by the Sun that reaches the Earth.
Shadow 影子 Ch 2Dark shape cast when an object blocks sunlight.
Shadow Length 影子的長度 Ch 2Measurement of how long a shadow is, changing with the Sun's height in the sky.
Shadow Direction 影子的方向 Ch 2Direction in which a shadow points, changing as the Earth rotates through the day.
Sundial 日晷 Ch 2Traditional instrument that tells time using shadows cast by sunlight.
Equator 赤道 Ch 2Imaginary line circling the middle of Earth, equidistant from North and South Poles.
Northern Hemisphere 北半球 Ch 2Half of the Earth north of the equator; Hong Kong lies in this hemisphere.
Southern Hemisphere 南半球 Ch 2Half of the Earth south of the equator.
Revolution 公轉 Ch 2Movement of the Earth around the Sun along a specific orbital path.
Revolve 公轉／繞行 Ch 2To travel around another body in an orbit, taking about 365 days (one year) for Earth.
Three Hundred Sixty-five Days 三百六十五天 Ch 2Approximate duration of one complete revolution of the Earth around the Sun (one year).
Orbit 軌道 Ch 2Curved path along which a planet like Earth revolves around the Sun.
Season 季節 Ch 2Division of the year marked by distinct weather, temperature, and daylight patterns.
Spring 春季 Ch 2Season following winter as temperatures gradually warm.
Summer 夏季 Ch 2Warmest season of a hemisphere when it receives more direct/overhead sunlight.
Autumn 秋季 Ch 2Season following summer as temperatures gradually cool.
Winter 冬季 Ch 2Coldest season of a hemisphere when sunlight strikes at a more slanted angle.
Overhead Sunlight 直射陽光 Ch 2Sunlight shining nearly straight down, concentrating heat and raising temperature.
Slanted Sunlight 斜射陽光 Ch 2Sunlight shining at an angle, spreading energy over a larger area with lower warmth.
Sunlight Angle 陽光照射角度 Ch 2Angle at which sunlight strikes Earth's surface, varying with latitude and season.
Longer Day 白晝較長 Ch 2Daylight period lasting longer than nighttime during summer in a hemisphere.
Shorter Night 黑夜較短 Ch 2Nighttime period shorter than daytime during summer in a hemisphere.
Equal Day and Night 晝夜平分／長短相若 Ch 2Condition near the equator where day and night lengths show little yearly change.
Arctic Ocean 北冰洋 Ch 2Ocean surrounding the North Pole, covered by sea ice throughout the year.
Antarctica 南極洲 Ch 2Continent around the South Pole and the coldest continent on Earth.
Sea Ice 海冰 Ch 2Frozen seawater floating on the ocean surface in polar regions.
West to East 自西向東 Ch 2Direction in which the Earth rotates on its axis.
Daylight 日光／白晝 Ch 2Natural light from the Sun during the daytime.
Timekeeping 計時 Ch 2Measuring and tracking time using clocks or sundials.
Tilted 傾斜的 Ch 2Sloping or leaning at an angle rather than straight upright.
Annual Cycle 周年循環 Ch 2Repeating one-year pattern of seasons tied to Earth's revolution.
Polar Circle 極圈 Ch 2High-latitude region near the North or South Pole.
Solar Energy 太陽能 Ch 2Light and heat energy coming from the Sun.

Classification 4: Weather & Climate (天氣與氣候)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Evaporation 蒸發 Ch 1Process by which liquid water turns into water vapour when warmed.
Condensation 凝結 Ch 1Process by which water vapour cools and turns back into liquid water droplets.
Higher Temperature 較高氣溫 Ch 2Warmer temperature often experienced when sunlight is more direct and focused.
Lower Temperature 較低氣溫 Ch 2Cooler temperature often experienced when sunlight is more slanted.
Weather 天氣 Ch 3Condition of the atmosphere—such as temperature, rain, and humidity—over a short time.
Climate 氣候 Ch 3Average weather condition of a place over a long period of time.
Atmosphere 大氣層 Ch 3Envelope of gases surrounding the Earth.
Temperature 氣溫／溫度 Ch 3Degree of hotness or coldness measured in degrees Celsius (°C).
Rainfall 雨量 Ch 3Amount of rain that falls in an area over a given period.
Relative Humidity 相對濕度 Ch 3Percentage of water vapour in the air compared to the maximum possible amount.
Water Vapour 水蒸氣 Ch 3Gaseous form of water present in the atmosphere.
Saturated Air 飽和空氣 Ch 3Air holding the maximum amount of water vapour possible at a given temperature.
Geographic Location 地理位置 Ch 3Where a place is situated on Earth, such as distance from the equator or hemisphere.
Hong Kong Observatory 香港天文台 Ch 3Official agency in Hong Kong monitoring weather, climate, and issuing warnings.
Very Hot Weather Warning 酷熱天氣警告 Ch 3Warning issued when temperatures reach 33°C or higher to prevent heatstroke.
Heatstroke 中暑 Ch 3Heat-related illness caused by prolonged exposure to high temperatures.
UV Index 紫外線指數 Ch 3Scale indicating the strength of ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.
Rainstorm 暴雨 Ch 3Heavy rainfall often accompanied by thunder, lightning, or strong winds.
Lightning 閃電 Ch 3Bright flash of electrical discharge in the sky during storms.
Climate Change 氣候變化 Ch 3Long-term shifts in average weather conditions such as temperature and rainfall.
Global Warming 全球增溫 Ch 3Long-term rise in Earth's surface temperature driven largely by human activities.
Greenhouse Gas 溫室氣體 Ch 3Atmospheric gas that traps heat radiating from Earth's surface.
Carbon Dioxide 二氧化碳 Ch 3Colourless, odourless gas released by burning fuels and breathing; a key greenhouse gas.
Coal 煤炭 Ch 3Black combustible fossil fuel burned for electricity and industry.
Oil 石油 Ch 3Liquid fossil fuel used for transport, heating, and manufacturing.
Fossil Fuel 化石燃料 Ch 3Energy sources like coal and oil whose excessive use increases greenhouse gases.
Melting Glacier 冰川融化 Ch 3Shrinking and melting of large ice masses due to rising global temperatures.
Rising Sea Level 海平面上升 Ch 3Increase in ocean water levels linked to warming oceans and melting ice.
Desertification 荒漠化 Ch 3Gradual loss of vegetation and fertile land turning into dry desert-like land.
Bushfire 山火 Ch 3Uncontrolled fire spreading through dry vegetation and brushland.
Industrial Development 工業發展 Ch 3Growth of factories and manufacturing that often increases energy and emission demands.
Transportation 交通運輸 Ch 3Movement of people and goods by vehicles, ships, or planes.
Hot Day 酷熱天氣日 Ch 3Day reaching high temperature thresholds tracked in climate records.
Cold Day 寒冷天氣日 Ch 3Day dropping to low temperature thresholds recorded by weather stations.
Annual Average 年平均值 Ch 3Mean value calculated across a full year, such as annual average sea level.
Wet Climate 濕潤氣候 Ch 3Climate characterized by higher moisture or relative humidity.
Dry Climate 乾燥氣候 Ch 3Climate with low rainfall or low moisture levels.
Rainy Season 雨季 Ch 3Time of year with noticeably higher rainfall.
Dry Season 旱季 Ch 3Time of year with relatively little rainfall.
Heat Trapping 阻隔熱量散失 Ch 3Effect where greenhouse gases prevent heat from escaping into space.
Air Temperature 空氣溫度 Ch 3Measure of warmth or coldness of the surrounding air.
Climate Record 氣候紀錄 Ch 3Historical measurements collected over decades to study climate trends.
Sea Level 海平面 Ch 3Average height of the ocean's surface.
Victoria Harbour 維多利亞港 Ch 3Harbour between Hong Kong Island and Kowloon where long-term sea levels are recorded.
Emission 排放 Ch 3Release of gases or substances into the atmosphere.
Atmospheric Condition 大氣狀況 Ch 3State of temperature, rain, wind, and humidity in the air at a given time.

Classification 5: Habitats & Adaptation (生境與適應)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Cactus 仙人掌 Ch 1Plant adapted to dry conditions and well-drained sandy soil.
Natural Environment 自然環境 Ch 4Natural surroundings with specific climate and geography where living things live.
Habitat 棲息地 Ch 4Place where an animal or plant naturally lives and gets what it needs.
Wetland 濕地 Ch 4Area at the boundary between land and water, including swamps and mangroves.
Temperate Grassland 溫帶草原 Ch 4Flat, open land with distinct rainy and dry seasons.
Polar Region 極地 Ch 4Very cold region around the North or South Pole covered with ice year-round.
Tropical Rainforest 熱帶雨林 Ch 4Very hot and rainy forest near the equator with abundant sunshine.
Desert 沙漠 Ch 4Dry habitat with little rainfall and large day-to-night temperature differences.
Swamp 沼澤 Ch 4Wetland area dominated by woody plants and saturated ground.
Mangrove 紅樹林 Ch 4Coastal wetland trees and shrubs adapted to salty or tidal mud.
Floodplain 氾濫平原 Ch 4Flat land next to a river that can be flooded occasionally.
Adapt 適應 Ch 4How living things use special body parts or behaviors to survive in an environment.
Adaptation 適應性／適應特徵 Ch 4Body feature or behavior that helps an organism survive in its habitat.
Thick Fur 厚毛 Ch 4Dense fur coat that helps animals like polar bears stay warm in cold places.
Layer of Fat 脂肪層 Ch 4Insulating fat under the skin that helps animals retain body heat.
Body Temperature 體溫 Ch 4Internal warmth level of a living organism.
Deep Winter Sleep 冬眠 Ch 4Deep sleep during cold winter to save energy when food and warmth are scarce.
Energy Consumption 能量消耗 Ch 4Amount of energy a living thing uses to stay alive and active.
Hedgehog 刺蝟 Ch 4Small spiny mammal that can go into a deep winter sleep in cold weather.
Polar Bear 北極熊 Ch 4Arctic mammal with thick fur and body fat adapted to freezing polar conditions.
Walrus 海象 Ch 4Large marine mammal of cold polar waters with a thick layer of fat under its skin.
Penguin 企鵝 Ch 4Flightless bird adapted to cold southern seas with a thick layer of fat.
Camel 駱駝 Ch 4Desert animal with long thick eyelashes, wide soft feet, and fat-storing humps.
Eyelash 眼睫毛 Ch 4Hair along the eyelid edge; long thick ones on camels help shield eyes from sand.
Wide and Soft Foot 寬大柔軟的腳掌 Ch 4Foot feature of camels that prevents sinking easily into loose desert sand.
Hump 駝峰 Ch 4Raised structure on a camel's back that stores fat.
Meerkat 狐獴 Ch 4Small animal that uses sharp claws to dig burrows underground for shelter.
Sharp Claw 尖銳的爪 Ch 4Pointed claws used for digging burrows or gripping.
African Spurred Tortoise 蘇卡達陸龜 Ch 4Desert tortoise with a hard waterproof shell that reduces water loss.
Waterproof Shell 防水硬殼 Ch 4Protective shell into which tortoises pull head and limbs to reduce water loss.
Jerboa 跳鼠 Ch 4Desert rodent that stays in a cave by day and searches for food at night.
Nocturnal Activity 晝伏夜出／夜間活動 Ch 4Being active at night to avoid daytime desert heat.
Long and Narrow Leaf 細長葉片 Ch 4Leaf shape in grassland plants that helps reduce water loss.
Wide Spreading Root 廣泛伸展的根 Ch 4Roots spreading widely to absorb more water from soil.
Needle-shaped Leaf 針狀葉 Ch 4Sharp, narrow leaf structure in desert plants that reduces water loss.
Thick Stem 粗莖 Ch 4Fleshy stem in plants like cacti used to store water.
Water Loss 水分流失 Ch 4Loss of water from a plant or animal body into dry or hot air.
Tall Tree 高大樹木 Ch 4High-growing rainforest trees reaching upward for abundant sunlight.
Climbing Plant 攀緣植物 Ch 4Plant whose stem climbs up tall trees for support to reach more sunlight.
Undergrowth 林下植物 Ch 4Plants growing beneath the forest canopy layer.
Broad Dark Green Leaf 寬闊深綠色葉片 Ch 4Leaf trait of rainforest undergrowth plants that helps capture sunlight.
Buttress Root 板根 Ch 4Large plank-like roots growing out from the base of a tree to support the trunk.
Tree Trunk 樹幹 Ch 4Main woody stem of a tree.
Migration 遷徙 Ch 4Seasonal movement of animals between places to escape cold, find food, or breed.
Migratory Bird 候鳥 Ch 4Bird that travels seasonally between different regions, such as wintering in Hong Kong.
Wintering Ground 越冬地 Ch 4Warmer place where migratory birds stay during cold winter months.
Reproduce 繁殖 Ch 4Process by which living things produce offspring.
Sunshine 日照／陽光 Ch 4Direct light from the Sun that plants use to make food.
Day and Night Difference 晝夜溫差 Ch 4Contrast between daytime and nighttime temperatures, notably large in deserts.
Survival 生存 Ch 4Continuing to live and stay alive in an environment.
Prop Root 支柱根 Ch 5Roots growing down from branches into the ground like pillars to support heavy trunks.
Pillar-like Root 支柱狀根系 Ch 5Sturdy downward roots acting like columns to prop up branches and trunks.

Classification 6: Ecology & Food Chains (生態與食物鏈)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Food Chain 食物鏈 Ch 5Sequence showing feeding relationships and energy flow from producers to consumers.
Feeding Relationship 攝食關係 Ch 5Connection formed when one living thing eats another in nature.
Producer 生產者 Ch 5Living thing (mainly plants) that absorbs sunlight to make its own food.
Make Food 製造養分／製造食物 Ch 5Process by which producers use sunlight and raw materials to produce food.
Plant-eating Animal 草食性動物 Ch 5Animal that eats only plants for food and energy (e.g., rabbit, deer, elephant).
Meat-eating Animal 肉食性動物 Ch 5Animal that eats other animals for food and energy (e.g., tiger, shark, eagle).
Plant-and-meat-eating Animal 雜食性動物 Ch 5Animal that eats both plants and animals (e.g., mouse, wild pig).
Predator 捕食者 Ch 5Animal that hunts other animals for food.
Prey 獵物 Ch 5Animal that is hunted and eaten by a predator.
Floating Plant 浮游植物 Ch 5Tiny algae floating in water that use sunlight to make food; key ocean producers.
Algae 藻類 Ch 5Simple plant-like organisms that make food using sunlight, found in water or moist places.
Overlapping Food Chain 重疊的食物鏈 Ch 5Food chains that share organisms, such as whales and sharks both hunting squid.
Ecosystem 生態系統 Ch 5Community of living things interacting with one another and their physical environment.
Ecological Environment 生態環境 Ch 5Natural environment where living organisms interact and maintain balance.
Ecological Balance 生態平衡 Ch 5Balanced state of kinds and numbers of living things in an ecosystem.
Lose Balance 失去平衡 Ch 5Disruption of an ecosystem when populations or habitats change sharply.
Depend on Each Other 相互依存 Ch 5Relationship where living things rely on and benefit from one another.
Compete with Each Other 相互競爭 Ch 5Relationship where organisms compete for limited resources to survive.
Compete for Food 爭奪食物 Ch 5Competition among living things for limited food supplies.
Compete for Sunlight 爭奪陽光 Ch 5Competition among plants for light needed to make food.
Living Space 生存空間／棲息空間 Ch 5Physical area and shelter needed by living things to live and grow.
Population Change 數量變化 Ch 5Increase or decrease in the number of a type of living thing in a food chain.
Grass 草 Ch 5Green producer plant eaten by herbivores like rabbits or deer in land food chains.
Rabbit 兔子 Ch 5Plant-eating animal in a forest food chain that eats grass and may be eaten by snakes.
Snake 蛇 Ch 5Carnivorous animal in a food chain that preys on smaller animals such as rabbits.
Eagle 鷹 Ch 5Bird of prey acting as a top meat-eating consumer in a land food chain.
Deer 鹿 Ch 5Plant-eating mammal that feeds on plants/grass and can be prey for tigers.
Tiger 老虎 Ch 5Large meat-eating predator in forest food chains.
Small Shrimp 小蝦 Ch 5Small aquatic consumer that feeds on floating plants in ocean food chains.
Squid 魷魚 Ch 5Marine animal that eats small shrimp and is hunted by sharks or whales.
Shark 鯊魚 Ch 5Large marine meat-eating predator near the top of ocean food chains.
Whale 鯨魚 Ch 5Large marine mammal that participates in ocean feeding relationships.
Nutrient Flow 養分流動 Ch 5Movement of nutrients from producers to consumers along a food chain.
Energy Transfer 能量傳遞 Ch 5Passing of energy from one organism to another when food is eaten.
Biodiversity 生物多樣性 Ch 5Variety of different species of animals, plants, and organisms in an environment.

Classification 7: Conservation & Lab (保育與實驗)

English Term 繁體中文 Chapter Definition / Key Notes
Building Material 建築材料 Ch 1Materials such as hard granite used in construction and buildings.
Sculpture 雕塑 Ch 1Three-dimensional artwork carved or shaped from stone or other materials.
Transparent Plastic Bag 透明塑膠袋 Ch 1Clear sealed bag used in the soil experiment to trap moisture escaping from soil.
Water Droplet 水滴 Ch 1Small bead of liquid water seen inside the bag after warming soil in sunlight.
Focused Light 較集中的光線 Ch 2Light concentrated into a smaller area, producing stronger heating in experiments.
Less Focused Light 較分散的光線 Ch 2Light spread across a wider area, resulting in lower heating intensity.
Torch Simulation 手電筒模擬實驗 Ch 2Classroom activity using a torch to model overhead and slanted sunlight on paper.
Deforestation 砍伐森林／毀林 Ch 3Clearing or cutting down large areas of forest trees.
Carbon Footprint 碳足跡／碳足印 Ch 3Amount of greenhouse gases or carbon dioxide produced by human activities.
Single-use Utensil 即棄餐具 Ch 3Disposable cutlery or foodware used once and discarded.
Food Waste 廚餘／浪費食物 Ch 3Food that is discarded uneaten, wasting resources and adding to emissions.
Energy Saving 節約能源 Ch 3Using less electricity and fuel to cut costs and lower greenhouse gas emissions.
Hydration 補充水分 Ch 3Drinking enough water to keep the body healthy, especially in hot weather.
Excessive Use 過度使用 Ch 3Using more of a resource or fuel than necessary.
Sustainable Habit 可持續習慣 Ch 3Daily practice that protects resources and reduces environmental harm.
Mai Po Nature Reserve 米埔自然護理區 Ch 4Important wetland reserve in Hong Kong providing food, shelter, and breeding space for birds.
Environmental Pollution 環境污染 Ch 5Harmful contamination of air, water, or soil that damages habitats and wildlife.
Hunting 狩獵／捕獵 Ch 5Catching or killing animals; excessive or illegal hunting can upset nature's balance.
Protect Nature 保護自然 Ch 5Taking care of natural habitats and wildlife so ecosystems stay healthy.
Wildlife 野生動物 Ch 5Animals living freely in natural environments rather than kept as tame pets.
Protected Wild Animal 受保護野生動物 Ch 5Wild animal species safeguarded by law against hunting, harm, or illegal trade.
Protected Wild Plant 受保護野生植物 Ch 5Wild plant species safeguarded by law against collection or trade.
Wildlife Product 野生動植物製品 Ch 5Items or goods made from wild animals or plants, which should not be bought.
Do Not Feed Wildlife 切勿餵飼野生動物 Ch 5Important rule protecting wild animals from unhealthy food and unnatural dependence.
Illegal Logging 非法砍伐 Ch 5Cutting down trees unlawfully, such as to sell timber for money.
Forest Destruction 破壞森林 Ch 5Damage or clearing of forest areas that removes homes for forest animals.
Habitat Loss 失去棲息地 Ch 5Disappearance of natural homes, causing animal numbers to decrease.
Report to the Police 報警處理 Ch 5Safe and appropriate action when witnessing illegal tree cutting or environmental crimes.
Human Behaviour 人類行為 Ch 5Actions taken by people that can either harm or help balance nature.
